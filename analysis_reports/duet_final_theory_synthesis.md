## Q1 — Why does CHORD (0.603) beat native DUET-v1 (0.549) on WebShop 1.5B?

**Short answer.** At cold start, DUET-v1's only teacher-gradient channel on specific expert tokens is DR3's density-ratio-corrected PPO surrogate; its **effective coefficient on the log-probability of a rare teacher token is advantage-and-ratio-weighted**, not unit. CHORD's SFT term delivers a **unit-coefficient, always-positive gradient on `log π_θ(a_teacher|s_teacher)`** that does not depend on `p_θ`, the trajectory advantage, or the discriminator. On WebShop 1.5B the expert-specific token (e.g., `click[155- yellow]`) lies in a near-disjoint region of the base model's action distribution, and that unit-coefficient gradient is the only reliable force that installs it within 100 steps.

**Mechanistic detail.** The DR3 teacher surrogate, as implemented at `het_core_algos.py:393-402` and `het_actor.py:1494-1500`, rewrites the teacher's "old log-prob" as `old_log_prob := log_prob.detach() - log(w_hat)`, so `ratio = w_hat` at the first gradient step. The per-teacher-token gradient from DR3 is

```
∂L_dr3 / ∂log π_θ(a*|s)  =  −A(τ) · clip(w_hat, 1-ε_c, 1+ε_c) · 1_{teacher}.
```

In probability space (what matters for "can the policy produce `click[155- yellow]` at inference"), applying softmax geometry gives `|Δp_θ| ≈ |A|·w_hat·p_θ·(1-p_θ)`. Three things must all be aligned for DR3 to lift a rare token: the trajectory advantage `A(τ)` must be positive (not guaranteed in a small GRPO group), the discriminator must produce a large-enough `w_hat` (but `disc_acc→1` causes the clip ceiling `clip_max=5.0` in v1 to bind, then the ratio becomes a bounded constant), and the softmax factor `p_θ(1-p_θ)` is small for ε-tokens. Result: at `p_θ=1e-4`, a single DR3 step lifts `p_θ` by `O(1e-4)` even with aligned sign and maximum `w_hat`.

The SFT term in CHORD (`compute_chord_sft_loss`, `het_core_algos.py:1758`) is simply `L_sft = −E_teacher[log π_θ(a*|s)]`. Its per-teacher-token gradient is

```
∂L_sft / ∂log π_θ(a*|s)  =  −μ · 1_{teacher},
```

so `|Δ log p_θ| ≈ μ`, independent of `p_θ`, `A`, or `w_hat`. Apply softmax geometry and `|Δp_θ| ≈ μ·p_θ·(1-p_θ)` per step — but because `log p_θ` climbs at constant rate `μ` per step, it reaches `p_θ = O(1)` in `O(log(1/ε)/μ)` steps regardless of advantage sign or discriminator state. With `μ=0.9` for the first 15 steps of CHORD's schedule, a token starting at `p_θ=1e-4` crosses `p_θ≈0.5` within ~10 optimizer steps. DR3 alone, even with `w_hat` pinned at `clip_max=5` and `A=+1`, would need ~50–100 aligned steps to achieve the same — and on 1.5B those steps are rarely simultaneously sign-aligned because GRPO groups mix teacher trajectories with varying success.

**Empirical validation of this mechanism.** The trajectory analysis in `webshop_1.5b_duet_trajectory_case_analysis.md` is decisive: the *color-match rate* (fraction of val trajectories that emit the exact SKU-token such as `155- yellow`, `a2-white`) is 10.9 % for v1, 1.6 % for v12 (DR3 stabilized), 28.3 % for CHORD, and 37.0 % for v24. The rank-correlation of color-match with val-score across the seven variants is >0.9. "Does the policy emit the rare teacher token at all?" explains almost the entire 5.4 pp gap between CHORD and v1.

**On the hypothesis the user asked to evaluate.** "CHORD has early strong unconditional token-level gradient on teacher tokens while DUET-v1's DR3 is advantage-weighted, trust-region-clipped" — **this is the correct mechanistic explanation.** Two refinements:
1. The clip is not actually the binding constraint in our logs (`off_pg_cliphit_rate=0` throughout all DUET variants; see `v25_divergence_analysis.md` §4). The correct statement is: DR3's effective coefficient is small because it multiplies `p_θ · (1-p_θ) · |A| · w_hat`, all of which co-suppress at cold start; not because the PPO clip is active.
2. CHORD's advantage over v1 is specifically in **getting the teacher-specific token into the policy support**. It is not an advantage in trajectory-level credit assignment — CHORD has no such mechanism at all. This matches Case C/D in the trajectory case study where CHORD loops 25× on `click[men]` because it never learned termination.

## Q2 — Why does v24 work, and will it generalize?

**The mechanism v24 uniquely provides (given what v25/v28/v29/v30/v33 ruled out).** With Framing C's "support lift" dead (v12 already has ~0.19–0.56 mass on option clicks; the issue is wrong-option-identity, not zero-support), and with "format stabilization" insufficient (v30 strong-KL stabilizes format but lands at 0.520), and with "w_hat variance control" insufficient (v28 EMA-tightened lands at 0.495, v33 soft-disc at 0.520, v29 combined at 0.511), the residual explanation is:

**BC delivers teacher-specific token identity, which neither DR3 nor any stability mechanism can provide.** This is subtly but importantly different from "BC lifts near-zero-probability tokens." Under the DR3 implementation, `w_hat` is a trajectory-level scalar — it carries no information about *which* token inside a teacher trajectory distinguishes teacher from on-policy. A DR3 gradient with `w_hat = 3` applied to a teacher trajectory multiplies every teacher-token's `log π_θ` gradient uniformly by that scalar. If the trajectory's `<think>...</think>` and generic `search[...]` prefix are already mastered by the policy, those tokens have high `p_θ` and absorb most of the probability-space gradient mass via the `p_θ(1-p_θ)` softmax factor; the rare, teacher-distinguishing `click[155- yellow]` token — at `p_θ=1e-4` — receives a much smaller absolute update even though it is, semantically, the token that most distinguishes teacher from student. **DR3 cannot prioritize teacher-distinguishing tokens because its ratio is sequence-level, not token-level.** SFT's per-token −log-prob gradient is automatically larger (in log-space) on tokens where `p_θ` is smaller; it is the *only* teacher-side operator in the DUET loss that is token-wise surprise-weighted by design.

KL-to-reference (v30) provides a per-token anchor but anchors to the *reference* distribution, not the teacher distribution — it can hold `<action>` grammar tokens in place (as v25's collapse shows is necessary) but it has no mechanism to push `click[155- yellow]` up. That is why v30 matches v12's training score but fails on token-level match (same as v12). This is the smoking-gun isolation: **BC is the only operator simultaneously (i) teacher-specific, (ii) per-token, and (iii) sign-positive at every step**. No combination of DR3 + stability + reward shaping reproduces all three properties.

The v25 collapse trajectory (`v25_trajectory_collapse.md`) gives the second half of the story: without BC, the on-policy PPO gradient is the dominant force shaping low-probability format tokens (`<action>`, `</action>`); after ~50 steps, the policy drifts these tokens off the SFT prior and emits `<story>`, nested `<action>`, or `<when>` garbage. BC, at even `μ=0.05`, pins these tokens via direct SFT pressure. So BC plays **two mechanistically distinct roles**: (A) teacher-specific token identity installation, dominant at high μ during steps 1–15, and (B) format-prior preservation, dominant at μ_valley=0.05 during steps 25–100. The curriculum story compresses these two into one weighted term, which is defensible but must not be oversold as a single "support lift" effect.

**Generalization predictions, argued from first principles.** BC's value scales as the product of (i) **rare-token mass gap**: how much of the teacher token distribution is at `p_θ < 1e-2` under the base model; (ii) **format-token fragility**: how easy it is for PPO to drift low-prob grammar tokens off the prior; (iii) **training budget**: more steps dilute the cold-start advantage but stress the format drift.

| Env × Scale | Rare-token gap | Format fragility | Prediction for v24-style BC |
|---|---|---|---|
| **ALFWorld 1.5B** | Low — templated actions (`take apple 1 from countertop 2`) already in base model distribution | Moderate — `<think>`/`<action>` grammar | **Marginal gain**, possibly neutral. v24-without-BC already beats CHORD at 1.5B (DUET 0.325 vs CHORD 0.27). Adding BC probably delivers +0-3 pp via format preservation alone. |
| **ALFWorld 3B** | Even lower | Lower | **Neutral to slight negative**. DUET-without-BC +8 pp over CHORD at 3B; adding BC on top risks over-imitating mid-training. The `μ_valley` tail probably helps; the peak μ=0.3 probably doesn't. |
| **ALFWorld 7B** | Very low | Very low | **Neutral**. 7B already hits 0.85 on-policy; teacher-side anything (BC, DR3, SC) provides marginal signal. BC specifically adds nothing because the rare-token gap it addresses is closed at scale. |
| **WebShop 1.5B** (known) | High — SKU-style option tokens absent from base | High — `<action>` grammar breaks easily | **Dominant** (+7.5 pp observed, replicated in this analysis). |
| **WebShop 3B** | Moderate — base 3B has better English-product language priors but still lacks SKU formats | Moderate | **Strong gain, probably +3-5 pp over CHORD**. Exact magnitude depends on 3B's prior mass on SKU-like tokens; we have no direct measurement. Prediction needs verification. |
| **WebShop 7B** | Low — 7B already emits `click[red]`-style option tokens unprompted | Low | **Marginal +1-2 pp**. The 7B LUFFY vs OnPolicy numbers on both envs (+0.015, negative in some buckets) show teacher mixing is nearly worthless at 7B. v24 does not rescue this. |

**The one prediction that is load-bearing for the paper**: v24-BC on ALFWorld 1.5B/3B should NOT underperform DUET-without-BC by more than ~2 pp. If it does (e.g., if BC pushes the ALFWorld policy into teacher-verbatim mode and hurts generalization on the procedural tail), the "Action Channel always contains a BC sub-term" framing collapses and we must accept BC is a WebShop-specific patch. *This is the single experiment that most cheaply validates the paper's framing and has not yet been run at the time of this writing.*

## Q3 — Can we preserve a unified dual-channel narrative?

**Proposed framing: "Action Channel = teacher-gradient curriculum (BC → DR3)"**, with the μ schedule serving as the curriculum parameter and State Channel unchanged. The paragraph that should appear in §3.1 of the paper is:

> *The Action Channel applies two complementary gradient operators to teacher trajectories. The first is a teacher-token-identity operator (behavior cloning) with per-token loss `−log π_θ(a_t^teacher | s_t)` weighted by a decaying schedule `μ_t: μ_0 → μ_∞`; it installs teacher-specific actions into the policy's support via per-token surprise weighting. The second is a trajectory-credit-assignment operator (DR3) using a density-ratio-corrected PPO surrogate with discriminator-estimated weights `w_hat(τ) = D(τ)/(1-D(τ))`, modulated by GRPO advantages; it refines the policy toward trajectories the expert executed well. The two are composed additively, `L_action = L_dr3 + μ_t · L_bc`, and together form a curriculum: BC dominates the teacher-side gradient when μ is large and the discriminator is still learning; DR3 dominates once the discriminator has converged and `w_hat` carries meaningful trajectory-level signal. Both operators retire naturally — BC via explicit schedule decay, DR3 via `w_hat → 1` as `π_θ → π_teacher` — giving the Action Channel a self-quiescing property on environments where the teacher–student gap is small.*

**Does the math back it up?** Yes, at the level of what each operator's per-token gradient actually does, but **no** at the level of the prior "support gap" story. Concretely:
- BC's gradient on `log π_θ(a*|s)` is unit-coefficient, sign-positive, unconditional; its effect on `p_θ` is `O(μ · p_θ · (1-p_θ))`. Its characteristic property is **teacher-token specificity**: the gradient magnitude is largest (in probability space) at tokens where the teacher is confident and the student is uncertain — which is exactly the `click[SKU]`-style rare token family.
- DR3's gradient on the same log-prob is `−A(τ) · clip(w_hat, …)`, which is (i) sign-sensitive to trajectory advantage, (ii) sequence-level in `w_hat`, and (iii) fades as `w_hat → 1`. Its characteristic property is **trajectory-level reweighting**: it reshapes which teacher trajectories contribute most, but it is token-uniform within each trajectory.
- These properties are orthogonal in the mathematical sense: you cannot derive one from the other via any reweighting. You need both if you want both teacher-specific token identity *and* trajectory-level credit assignment. Crucially, CHORD has only the first, on-policy GRPO has only the second (with no teacher trajectory processing at all), and DUET-v1 has only the second. v24 has both.

**Defensibility against reviewers.** The framing avoids the three failure modes the agent team identified for Framing C (`framing_C_agent_team_verdict.md`): (i) no bogus Theorem 1 claim about absolute-`p_θ` cold-start bounds; (ii) no claim of "automatic specialization by `p_θ`" which the empirics refute; (iii) no downgrading of SC to an appendix. It does not hide μ_valley — it explicitly says the curriculum "retires" BC via the schedule, admitting that μ never reaches zero. If asked directly "why is μ_valley = 0.05, not 0?", we answer: "To preserve the teacher-identity prior against format drift observed when μ = 0 (v25 experiment); this is a regularization sub-role of the same operator, not a separate channel." This is more honest than Framing C and matches the trajectory evidence from v25's collapse.

**Anticipated reviewer critiques and rebuttals.**
- *"This is just CHORD + DR3."* — CHORD is a specific pairing with a hand-tuned schedule and no density-ratio correction on the PG term. We add DR3's density-ratio correction (principled off-policy PG under black-box teacher log-probs) and an automatic discriminator-based fade-out. Neither is in CHORD. The v22/v23 ablations (constant μ CHORD on top of DR3) show raw CHORD scheduling is insufficient; v24's decayed schedule is load-bearing. The unified-operator framing is not a re-derivation of CHORD; it is the positioning that makes the DR3+schedule pairing theoretically motivated rather than empirical.
- *"Why two operators, not one (AWAC, exponential-advantage-weighted BC)?"* — AWAC provides advantage-modulated BC; our DR3 operator additionally provides density-ratio correction and an automatic curriculum via `w_hat → 1`. A reasonable paper response is to include AWAC as a baseline. If AWAC matches v24, we concede the two-operator framing is a design convenience and reposition as "DR3-curriculum is more analyzable than AWAC's single-term coupling." If AWAC underperforms, the two-operator framing is vindicated. *This baseline has not been run.*
- *"The State Channel ablation is load-bearing (v4: -26 pp), but your framing puts SC as a secondary contribution."* — SC is a primary contribution; the paper presents two channels. The Action Channel contains two operators; the State Channel contains one (`β · Φ(τ)`). This is precisely the dual-channel structure — and the two-operator Action Channel does not dilute it, because BC and DR3 share a subject (teacher-action correction) that is distinct from SC's subject (state-visitation shaping).
- *"Your μ_valley exposes that BC has two roles."* — Yes, and we document both: cold-start bootstrap (steps 1–15 where μ ≥ 0.2) and format-prior preservation (steps 25+ where μ = 0.05). v30's failure (strong KL alone lands at 0.520) shows KL-to-ref cannot substitute for role B, and v25's collapse shows role B is not optional. This is ablation-supported, not hand-waved.

**Is this more honest than Framing C?** Unambiguously yes. Framing C made three specific claims that turned out to be empirically or mathematically false: (1) automatic `p_θ`-specialization — refuted by `corr(μ, tgs) = +0.64 >> |corr(disc_acc, tgs)| = 0.49`; (2) minimum-two-operator necessity — refuted by AWAC-style alternatives the math doesn't exclude; (3) vacuous combined lower bound at cold start. The curriculum framing sidesteps all three: it makes no specialization claim, no necessity claim, and proves nothing about cold-start probability progress. It simply describes what the loss function is, says each operator addresses a different gradient-type need, and reports the empirical specialization that *does* exist in the data (μ schedule dominates teacher-gradient share through 25 steps; DR3 dominates through 50–100 steps because `w_hat > 1` and μ → 0.05). That is defensible because it is a description of code and logs, not a theoretical claim.

## Recommended one-paragraph summary (internalize before meeting)

DUET-v1 loses to CHORD on WebShop 1.5B because its only teacher-gradient operator (DR3) is a *sequence-level density-ratio correction multiplying trajectory advantage* — it cannot prioritize teacher-specific tokens (like `click[155- yellow]`), which on 1.5B start at `p_θ < 1e-4`; CHORD's unit-coefficient per-token SFT gradient installs those tokens in ~10 optimizer steps while DR3 would need ~100 aligned-sign steps. v24 works because BC uniquely provides three properties DR3 and no stability mechanism (KL, soft discriminator, w_hat EMA) can replicate simultaneously: teacher-specificity, per-token surprise weighting, and unconditional positive sign; μ_valley = 0.05 additionally preserves format tokens that on-policy PPO would otherwise drift (v25 collapse). The dual-channel narrative survives if we reframe the Action Channel as a *teacher-gradient curriculum with two complementary operators* — BC for teacher-token identity, DR3 for trajectory-level credit assignment — with μ_t as the curriculum schedule; this is honest because it describes what the code does, defensible because each operator maps to a distinct mathematical property, and falsifiable via the ALFWorld-with-BC ablation (the single experiment still owed to the paper).

## Caveats and unverified claims (flagged explicitly)

- **ALFWorld-with-BC generalization prediction** (Q2 table, ALFWorld rows) is first-principles reasoning from rare-token mass and format fragility. The specific +0-3 pp range has *not* been empirically validated. If v24 on ALFWorld 1.5B underperforms DUET-without-BC, the curriculum framing must be demoted to "WebShop-specific Action Channel variant."
- **WebShop 3B/7B v24 predictions** (Q2 table) are extrapolated from 1.5B mechanism + observed teacher-gap-collapse at 7B. Not directly measured.
- **AWAC comparison** (Q3) — without running AWAC as baseline, the "minimality" defense of two-operator framing is weakened. Framing can survive without AWAC results but is more robust with them.
- **BC's dual role quantification** (Q2) — we have ablation evidence for the format-preservation role (v25 collapse) and for the identity-installation role (trajectory case study), but not a per-step decomposition of which role dominates at which step. A figure plotting `p_θ` for `<action>` grammar tokens vs `click[SKU]` semantic tokens across training would make this decomposition visible and should go in the appendix.
