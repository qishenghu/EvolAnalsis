## Why these settings (paper-ready mechanism)

**v39 family adopts a closed-form `μ(t)` driven by a learned discriminator** (see Section 1 of the handoff):
```
μ_raw(t) = valley + (peak − valley) × max(0, (1 − d̄(t)) / (1 − d_floor))
```
where `d̄(t)` is the EMA of the discriminator's accuracy at separating teacher vs on-policy samples. The discriminator is shared with the DR3 density-ratio estimator — one learning signal drives BOTH the IS correction and BC scheduling.

### ALFWorld: the d_ema_α=0.5 + d_floor=0.4 cell wins

ALFWorld's per-step rewards are deterministic (subtask completion), so the on-policy gradient direction is reliable. The model can absorb teacher behavior quickly; standard valley=0.05 with default cliff timing is optimal. d_floor=0.4 keeps cliff slightly earlier than default 0.5, freeing the policy to explore on-policy sooner.

### WebShop: late + soft cliff is necessary

Per `analysis_reports/v39_vs_v24_webshop_diagnosis.md`, WebShop's noisier reward (binary terminal success + continuous shopping reward) makes the on-policy gradient direction unreliable during the BC removal "cliff" (around step 25–32). With `valley=0.05, d_floor=0.5`, the policy loses BC anchor right when reward is climbing and entropy collapses (or explodes) — train succ@100 = 0%, val@100 = 5.5%.

Two mutually-reinforcing levers fix this:
1. **`valley=0.10`** keeps BC weight nontrivial even after cliff (weighted_sft_loss ≈ 2× higher in late training)
2. **`d_floor=0.6`** delays cliff start until d̄ ≥ 0.6, giving the policy 5–10 more steps with full BC

Together: train succ@100 = 12% → val@100 = 36.0%. Beats prev hand-tuned SOTA by 14pp.
