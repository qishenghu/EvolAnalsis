   这更贴近你的本意：不是为了“分解”而分解，而是为了更充分利用 teacher trajectories。

5. **Action / State Channel 的句子要少用 “organizes around this distinction” 这种抽象表达**。可以更直接：

6. **density-ratio discriminator 的描述应同时包含 distribution correction 和 attenuation**。现在只强调 attenuation，容易让人觉得只是 schedule replacement。更准确的写法是：

## Revised Introduction

Large language model (LLM) agents are increasingly trained with reinforcement learning (RL) to solve interactive tasks that require multi-turn decision making in external environments [Shridhar et al., 2020, Yao et al., 2022, ?]. Group-based policy-gradient methods such as GRPO [Shao et al., 2024] have become a practical backbone for this training. However, these methods remain difficult to use in sparse-reward settings. When a weak student agent rarely completes a task on its own, the on-policy rollout batch contains little positive signal, and the policy gradient has almost nothing to reinforce.

A natural way to address this cold-start problem is to replay successful trajectories from a stronger teacher model. Recent methods follow this idea with promising results. LUFFY [Yan et al., 2025] mixes teacher demonstrations into GRPO rollout groups, while CHORD [?] combines teacher-mixed RL with a weighted imitation objective. These methods show that teacher data can substantially accelerate LLM-agent training. Most existing uses of teacher replay, however, treat the teacher trajectory mainly as an action sequence: the teacher’s outputs either enter the policy-gradient batch or serve as supervised targets.

This view leaves part of the teacher trajectory unused. In an interactive environment, a successful trajectory contains both the teacher’s actions and the states that those actions reach. The actions show one way to solve the task. The resulting observations reveal what task progress looks like. These two signals are related but not identical: a student may benefit from knowing that a state is closer to success without being forced to imitate the exact action that the teacher took to get there. This distinction is especially important for LLM agents, where different reasoning traces or action sequences can lead to the same successful outcome.

At the same time, even action-level teacher replay is not statistically benign in GRPO-style training. If teacher and student rollouts are normalized together, two problems appear. First, teacher rewards can contaminate the group baseline. Since replayed teacher trajectories are typically successful, their high rewards raise the group reward statistics and can reduce the advantages assigned to the few successful on-policy student rollouts. This weakens precisely the exploratory behavior that RL should reinforce. Second, teacher trajectories are off-policy. In principle, their gradients should be corrected by a teacher–student importance ratio. In practice, exact correction is often unavailable: the teacher may be a closed-source model, its token probabilities may not be logged, or its tokenizer and action representation may not align with the student’s. Directly replaying teacher trajectories therefore leaves an uncorrected distribution mismatch, and the teacher can continue to dominate training after its guidance is no longer appropriate. Both problems are most severe in the cold-start regime, where teacher replay is most useful.

We propose DUET, a teacher-replay framework that uses teacher trajectories as two complementary sources of signal. The Action Channel determines how teacher outputs affect the policy update. It separates teacher and on-policy baselines to prevent teacher rewards from distorting on-policy credit assignment, and uses a discriminator-based density-ratio weight to approximate the missing teacher–student correction when exact likelihood ratios are unavailable. This weight also reduces the impact of teacher-action gradients as the student improves, so teacher influence is controlled by the estimated distribution gap rather than by a fixed training-step schedule. In parallel, the State Channel uses teacher observations to build a progress map over environment states. This map provides dense reward shaping for on-policy rollouts, guiding exploration toward teacher-like progress without requiring the student to copy teacher actions.

The two channels therefore use different parts of the same teacher data for different purposes. Teacher actions provide corrected action-level supervision, while teacher states provide progress-based exploration guidance. Because the State Channel shapes only on-policy rollouts and the Action Channel corrects teacher-action gradients, DUET can combine both signals without reintroducing the baseline contamination that arises from naive teacher mixing.

We evaluate DUET on ALFWorld and WebShop using Qwen2.5-1.5B/3B-Instruct students and a fixed Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct teacher cache. DUET achieves the highest success rate among all reproduced baselines in all four model-environment settings. The improvements are largest for the weaker 1.5B students, where cold start is most severe: DUET improves over the strongest prior baseline by 17.5 percentage points on both ALFWorld and WebShop. Ablations show that baseline separation is essential for stable training, while the density-ratio correction and State Channel provide complementary gains.

Our contributions are:

1. We identify two estimator-level problems in teacher-mixed GRPO: baseline contamination from high-reward teacher rollouts and distribution mismatch from off-policy teacher trajectories whose likelihoods may be unavailable.

2. We propose DUET, a teacher-replay framework that uses teacher actions and teacher states through separate mechanisms: corrected action-level learning and progress-based reward shaping.

3. We introduce a discriminator-based density-ratio correction for teacher-action gradients, together with baseline separation, to make teacher replay more compatible with group-normalized policy-gradient updates.

4. We show empirically that DUET improves LLM-agent RL across two interactive environments and two student model scales, with the largest gains in the cold-start regime and ablations validating each component.
