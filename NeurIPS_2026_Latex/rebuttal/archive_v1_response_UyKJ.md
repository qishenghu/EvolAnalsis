**On how performance changes when the teacher is weaker.**
Teacher quality enters DUET only through the *relative* teacher–student gap, logged every step as the mean per-group difference between teacher and on-policy reward. Holding the cache fixed and changing only the student produces exactly this regime. On ALFWorld the gap is 0.93 early / 0.55 late at 1.5B, but 0.42 / 0.06 at 7B; on 10 of 100 steps the 7B student *beats* the 72B teacher (min −0.10), which never happens at 1.5B (min +0.38). DR3 responds without being told: at an identical 12.2% mixing rate, the teacher's share of the policy gradient falls from a run-mean of 0.241 (1.5B) to 0.079 (7B) — 3.05× lower overall — while the applied teacher weight $\hat w$ starts at $\approx 1.0$ and drops to a run median of 0.58 (min 0.23) as the discriminator separates the two distributions. The outcome is the point: at 7B, DUET reaches 86.5% vs GRPO's 85.0% (+1.5pp), whereas LUFFY — same teacher, cache and mixing rate, no fade-out — drops *below* the no-teacher baseline, to 82.5% on ALFWorld (−2.5pp) and 45.5% vs 50.0% strict SR on WebShop (−4.5pp), routing 1.71× more teacher gradient than DUET. DUET's benefit is thus proportional to the measured gap (+46.5pp over GRPO at 1.5B, +1.5pp at 7B): when the teacher stops being useful DUET degrades to GRPO, while methods without fade-out are actively harmed. We will promote this into an explicit teacher-relative-strength analysis.

**On the Pick Two exception.**
*First, the cell is not statistically resolvable.* Pick Two is n=45 of 200 validation tasks (verified against ALFWorld ground-truth types). All methods see the same tasks, so the correct test is paired: DUET and GRPO agree on 27 and disagree on 18 (6 DUET-only, 12 GRPO-only) — exact McNemar p=0.24, with near-fully overlapping Wilson intervals. For calibration, three seed replicates of one *identical* configuration span 8.9pp on this same subset (1.5B replicates; we have no 3B seed replicates). The effect also does not replicate off that cell: at 1.5B DUET gets 24.4% vs GRPO 0.0%, at 7B 77.8% vs 75.6%, and on 3B *training* rollouts DUET solves 41.7% of Pick Two vs GRPO's 34.6% — a Pick-Two-minus-overall deficit that is method-independent (−20.0 DUET, −20.3 GRPO, −16.6 LUFFY).

*Second, there is nonetheless a real reason the State Channel helps least here, which we will state as a limitation.* Pick Two demonstrations are the longest (14.7 observations) and by far the most self-revisiting — 52.6% contain a repeated normalized state vs 7.5–30.8% elsewhere. Because $\Phi(s)$ is the *maximum* normalized index at which a state occurs, this self-similarity makes the potential saturate inside the first sub-goal: $\Phi \ge 0.9$ is reached at 60% of the demonstration (79–86% for other manipulation types). This is **not** a coverage failure — hit rate on Pick Two is the highest of any type (0.81) and stays high after the first delivery — but a dynamic-range failure: mean $\Phi$ rises only 0.49→0.61 across the entire second-object phase, vs +0.29 for Pick&Place. The shaped signal therefore barely separates outcome: SC progress on *failed* rollouts is 0.414 (vs 0.216–0.260), the success/failure gap 0.14 (vs 0.27–0.32), AUC 0.71 (vs 0.83–0.89), and Pick Two is the only type where failed rollouts receive net-positive shaped reward (+0.33). The behaviour matches: on the 12 tasks GRPO solved and DUET did not, 11/12 DUET trajectories picked up exactly one object and kept searching *while still carrying it*; all 12 GRPO trajectories delivered first. Since $\Phi \approx 0.8$ once the first object is in hand, shaping does not discourage this, and DUET averages 24.0 actions per Pick Two episode vs GRPO's 19.0 — despite being *more* efficient on every other type (13.9 vs 14.6). The fix is a task-structure-aware potential (sub-goal-indexed progress, or the soft matcher we already use for WebShop), not a change to the Action Channel. We will add the n=45 caveat, this mechanism, and the appendix figure's exact run identity; all tables and figures are being regenerated under one stated evaluation protocol.

**On whether the discriminator separates teacher from student, or just success from failure.**
Directly tested — it is not a success detector. We trained probes on the DR3 feature family (assistant-token log-prob mean/std/min, low-probability tail ratios, log length; reward, advantage and success are never inputs) over 1.5B ALFWorld rollouts. If the signal were "success vs failure," restricting the student side to *successes only* would destroy it. It does not: accuracy is 90.0% (all steps), 98.2% (early), 90.0% (late) — at or above the unrestricted probe. And late in training, where the student already succeeds on 42% of rollouts, successful and failed student rollouts get near-identical P(student) (0.861 vs 0.869) while teacher trajectories sit at 0.280. Caveat: this is an offline probe reproducing DR3's feature family and objective under a fixed policy snapshot, not a readout of the online discriminator's parameters — it tests whether the information *available* to DR3 supports a success shortcut, which is your question.

**On DR3's theoretical justification.**
Auditing the code for this response we found that our own Eq. 8/9 misdescribe the implementation, and correcting them makes the justification stronger. (i) The applied weight is not $D/(1-D)$ but the standard **relative** density ratio $\hat w_\alpha = \hat r/((1-\alpha)\hat r + \alpha)$ against the *mixture*, where $\alpha$ is the teacher fraction of the buffer. It is empirically bounded by $1/(1-\alpha) \approx 1.13$, so DR3 can only ever *down-weight* a teacher sample, never amplify one — precisely the fade-out in §4.4, and a far better variance profile than an unbounded $p/q$. (ii) Eq. 9's product $\hat w \cdot \rho_t$ is wrong notation: for teacher samples the code *replaces* the behaviour log-probability (`old_lp = log_prob.detach() − log ŵ`), so the single ratio in the clipped surrogate evaluates to $\hat w_\alpha$ exactly, with gradient $\hat w_\alpha \nabla_\theta \log \pi_\theta$ — one correction, not two stacked. We will rewrite both equations. We also *keep* the "bias-mitigating replay weight" framing you flag: because the cache is success-filtered, $\hat w_\alpha$ is a bounded, sequence-level correction estimated on a rolling buffer, and we prefer to justify it by measured behaviour (fade-out tracking the gap; blindness to success) than to claim an exactness the success filter rules out.

**On hyperparameter burden.** Fair, with one clarification: the two core channels are *not* tuned per setting. SC ($\beta=0.2$, $\eta=0.05$, `exclude_teacher`) and DR3 (`disc_lr` 3e-4, `buffer_size` 1024) are identical across all six main-table DUET configs (1.5B/3B/7B × ALFWorld/WebShop). The sensitivity in Appendix B is concentrated in the **BC cold-start coefficient** $\mu(t)$ — genuinely sharp on 1.5B-WebShop ($d_{\text{floor}}$ 0.6→0.5 halves success, →0.4 collapses it) — which is a safety net, not one of the two channels. We will say this explicitly rather than imply the method is tuning-free.
