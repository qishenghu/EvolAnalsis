# Response to Reviewer y9x6

Thank you — this review named the claims we could not support as stated. We add: paired significance tests on the cells we cite plus multi-seed runs in progress; a direct measurement of the state-matching failure mode under observation noise; a diagnostic testing whether the DR3 discriminator is a success detector; full cache statistics, with cache-size and teacher-quality ablations running; and the $-$BC ablation as the attribution answer. Where we lack the experiment you asked for, we say so.

**On generalisation to noisier, open-ended, partially observable environments.** State matching is confined to one of four components. Teacher-separated baselines, DR3 and adaptive BC read only *(teacher token ids, teacher/on-policy mask, scalar reward)* — no observations, no environment structure, no teacher log-probabilities, no shared tokenizer. That is byte-for-byte CHORD's input: wherever CHORD runs, DUET's core runs unchanged. Second, SC is an interface $\Phi(s)\in[0,1]$, not one matcher. ALFWorld uses the teacher-derived hash map (3 lines of per-environment code); **our WebShop runs use no state matching at all** — $\Phi$ comes from page/attribute content — so the dependency you describe does not apply to half of our main table. Its cost is different, and we will state it rather than claim a free recipe: the WebShop potential is ~210 lines of hand-written domain logic.

We also measured the failure mode instead of arguing about it, by adding word-level dropout to *the matcher's copy* of the observation (the policy's input is untouched, so task difficulty is held fixed) plus a dependency-free TF-IDF matcher selected by one config field. On the real 72B ALFWorld cache (300 tasks; map built from all but one demo, queried with the held-out demo), at 30% noise exact hashing retains 6.6% of the clean progress signal while soft matching retains 101% (at 50% noise, 1.4% vs 87%). Caveat: at $\theta$=0.6 about 34% of cross-task queries also match. **The end-to-end runs have now finished, and the three-way contrast answers your question directly.** All three train DUET on the paper's own 800 tasks and are scored on the same 200 validation tasks; only the State Channel's lookup operator and the observations the *matcher* sees differ. With 30% observation noise and **exact** matching, state coverage falls from 0.590 to 0.178 and strict success falls to **11.0%** — *below* the 31.0% we get by removing the State Channel entirely. Your concern is therefore right, and in a sharper form than posed: a matcher that fails is not merely uninformative but actively harmful, because states genuinely on the expert path are scored $\Phi=0$ and the shaping becomes systematically misleading. With the same noise and a **soft** matcher — one config field, the same progress map, no new dependency, no learned model — coverage recovers to 0.654 and strict success to **54.5%**, against the paper's clean exact-matching 47.5%. The dependence you identify is real and it lives in the lookup operator, not in the channel; replacing that operator costs nothing and removes it. We read the 54.5-vs-47.5 gap as "at least as good" rather than "better", since our three-seed spread on a comparable 1.5B-ALFWorld cell has sd 4.9pp. If $\Phi$ cannot be built at all, DUET degrades rather than breaks on ALFWorld — with SC disabled, 31.0% vs CHORD 27.0% (paired McNemar $p$=0.31, i.e. parity), retaining 64.5% of DUET's gain over GRPO. On 1.5B-WebShop that ablation is *not* favourable to us: 1.0% strict success, below CHORD's 11.5%. There, SC is load-bearing, and we will say so.

**On DR3 as a principled estimator versus a learned heuristic.** You are right, and our notation overstated it. What is applied is the bounded $\alpha$-relative ratio $\hat w=\hat r/((1-\alpha)\hat r+\alpha)$ with $\hat r=D_\phi/(1-D_\phi)$ and measured $\alpha\approx0.10$–$0.12$, so $\hat w\le1.13$: DR3 can only ever *down-weight* teacher samples, never amplify them — a variance-controlled replay weight, not an exact likelihood ratio. Eq. 8 prints the intermediate $\hat r$ and Eq. 9 misdescribes our own code (teacher samples receive exactly one correction, by imputing the missing behaviour log-probability, not a product of two ratios); both are corrected in the revision, and we soften "principled estimator" to "bounded surrogate with a measured non-confound". Your specific mechanism is testable, so we tested it: probes trained on DR3's own reward- and advantage-free features, labelled student-vs-teacher, do *not* weaken when conditioned on success ($D_{\text{succ}}$ = 90.0% over all steps, 98.2% early). Late in training, when the student already succeeds on 42% of rollouts, successful and failed student rollouts receive near-identical $P(\text{student})$ — 0.861 vs 0.869 — while teacher trajectories sit at 0.280. The discriminator is close to blind to outcome. This is an offline probe reproducing DR3's feature family under a fixed policy snapshot, not a readout of the online discriminator; it goes into the appendix with that caveat.

**On whether the gains come from the extra imitation signal rather than from separation and correction.** We ran the isolating ablation: remove BC entirely. At 1.5B, DUET$-$BC gives 34.0% (ALFWorld) / 16.5% (WebShop) against full DUET 47.5%/36.0% and GRPO 1.0%/0.5% — with *no imitation term at all*, ~71%/~45% of DUET's gain survives. Against the baselines whose entire mechanism *is* imitation on the same cache, DUET$-$BC is at parity or better: vs SFT+GRPO $p$=0.34 (AF) and $p$=0.72 (WS); vs CHORD 34.0 vs 27.0 ($p$=0.076) and 16.5 vs 11.5 ($p$=0.031). Conversely BC does not carry the method: keeping BC and removing DR3 costs 26.5pp on WebShop (36.0% → 9.5%). And full DUET's margin over the strongest imitation baseline is not marginal — paired exact McNemar $p$=2.2e-5 (AF, 51 vs 16 discordant), $p$=7.9e-8 (WS, 40 vs 5).

**On single-seed results and confidence intervals.** Conceded; two distinct variances are at issue. *Evaluation-set* variance is quantifiable now, since every method is evaluated on the same 200 tasks — the paired tests above show the headline margins are not an artifact of task sampling. *Training-seed* variance is what your question targets, and the paired test says nothing about it. The only three-seed set we currently hold is a 1.5B-ALFWorld DUET variant ($-$DR3, which scores identically to full DUET at that scale): 47.5/38.0/41.0%, mean 42.2%, sd 4.9pp — the reported 17.5pp margin is ≈3.6× that sd, and the worst seed still exceeds the strongest baseline (30.0%) by 8.0pp. Running now on two machines: ALFWorld DUET seeds 2025/2026/2027, WebShop DUET seeds 2025/2027, and SFT+GRPO seeds 2025/2027 so the baseline is replicated too. We will report mean ± sd here and in the camera-ready.

**The first replicate has landed, it is not favourable to us, and we report it now — together with a methodological correction we found while diagnosing it.** On 1.5B-WebShop, seed 2025 reaches **3.5%** strict success at val@100 against the submitted seed's 35.5%. Investigating that gap surfaced something we should have caught earlier: in our code the seed does *two* jobs, because it also shuffles the training split from which the first `max_train_tasks` are kept. At a 100-step budget a run only ever sees 800 tasks, so seeds 2026 and 2025 trained on largely **different curricula** — 89 of 800 tasks in common on WebShop (ALFWorld, with a smaller pool, shares ~265 of 800, which matches its much smaller spread). We have now separated the two: `data.task_seed` pins the task draw while `data.seed` continues to vary run-time randomness, and the like-for-like replicates (same 800 tasks as the submitted run, only randomness differing) are running; we will report those as the seed variance and the curriculum-varying numbers separately as the end-to-end variance of the protocol. Two things this does **not** affect: validation tasks are loaded unshuffled and seed-independently, so all runs are scored on the same fixed 200 tasks; and every method in Table 1 uses the same seed, which we verified from the saved rollouts — CHORD and on-policy GRPO trained on *exactly* the same 800 tasks as DUET, and SFT+GRPO's 400 are a subset of them.

Two hypotheses we tested and ruled out before concluding the above. The environment was not at fault: the seed-2025 run has 7 validation episodes with unparseable trajectories, and dumping them shows they are degenerate repetitions in the *policy's* output (`search[... small small small ...]` to the token limit), each correctly penalised at −0.1; its overall failure rate is 11.0% against 5.0% for seed 2026 and 58.5% for on-policy GRPO, with normal episode lengths. Teacher matching was not at fault either: measured against each run's actual task set, 85.8% versus 83.5% of tasks have at least one demonstration (3.97 versus 3.82 demonstrations per task), and the realised teacher rate per prompt was 0.857 versus 0.834. The cause is diagnosable rather than mysterious. On-policy training success is still *rising* at step 100 for both seeds, with seed 2025 roughly ten steps behind (final 10-step block .084 vs .125, which matches seed 2026 at steps 81–90), and WebShop's strict metric requires an exact match on every requested attribute. Seed 2025 leaves 32.5% of its evaluation episodes stranded in the 0.75–0.90 band that seed 2026 converted into exact matches (20.5% there), so a modest difference in policy quality is amplified into a 32-point difference in *strict* success. On mean reward over the same episodes the two seeds are 0.706 and 0.521, both far above on-policy GRPO's 0.152; CHORD shows the same signature (0.603 mean but 11.5% strict, 37.5% stranded in that band), which points to a property of 1.5B-WebShop at a 100-step budget rather than of DUET. Two runs will settle it: the third seed, and the seed-2025 configuration with the budget extended to 150 steps, which separates "slower transition" from "worse policy". The extended run will be reported *alongside* the protocol-matched 100-step number, never in place of it, and we will not quote a best-seed number for this cell.

**On cache size, diversity, quality, and success filtering.** ALFWorld: 19,497 successful Qwen2.5-72B trajectories over 2,348 tasks (8.3/task), filtered from 24,200 raw rollouts at reward 1.0 (80.6% kept). WebShop: 26,178 over 5,691 tasks. Diversity is not degenerate: on ALFWorld a task's demonstrations hold on average 4.4 *distinct* action sequences out of 8.5 and only 7.6% of tasks collapse to one repeated path; on WebShop no two demonstrations of a task share an action sequence (0% of 49,865 pairs). A provenance fact we should have stated: the ALFWorld cache is sampled 72B rollouts, success-filtered, whereas the WebShop cache is verified gold action sequences with 72B-authored rationales (hence its 100% keep rate) — so our environments already span a realistic noisy teacher and an oracle one, with the same ranking in both. Scale is not the mechanism: a 100-step run consumes ≈781 trajectories, 4.0%/3.0% of the caches. *Cache size:* 10% (1,950 trajectories, 55.2% task coverage) and 1% (195, 7.8%) runs are in progress; subsampling costs coverage before depth, so at 1% roughly 92% of training groups would hold no demonstration and DUET degrades toward GRPO with baseline separation.

*Teacher quality:* we do not yet have a weaker teacher model — a Qwen2.5-14B cache is being collected and we will report it if it lands in time. Quality enters DUET only through the *relative* teacher–student gap, which we log every step. Holding the cache fixed and changing only the student, that gap is 0.932/0.549 (early/late) at 1.5B but 0.420/0.063 at 7B, and at 7B the student beats the 72B teacher on 10 of 100 steps (min $-$0.101), never at 1.5B (min +0.384). DR3 reacts unprompted: at an identical 12.2% mixing rate the teacher's gradient share has run-mean 0.241 at 1.5B vs 0.079 at 7B. At 7B, DUET reaches 86.5% vs GRPO 85.0%, while LUFFY — same cache, same mixing rate, no fade-out, 1.71× more teacher gradient — falls *below* the no-teacher baseline (82.5% ALFWorld; 45.5% vs 50.0% WebShop). *Mixing ratio:* the nominal rate is fixed (1 teacher rollout per group of 8) but DR3 makes the effective rate adaptive over an order of magnitude within a run (0.166 → 0.013 at 7B); removing the correction at fixed nominal rate costs 26.5pp on WebShop. A 2-per-group (25%) run is queued as an optional cell.

**On broader off-policy baselines, alternative corrections, and simpler shaping.** Fair — we cover on-policy GRPO, mixing with policy shaping (LUFFY), weighted SFT inside RL (CHORD) and SFT→GRPO, but not the classical off-policy/replay family. Two controlled comparisons at fixed data and mixing rate: *uncorrected* replay ($-$DR3) gives 47.5%/9.5%, and LUFFY's $\pi_\theta/(\pi_\theta+\beta)$ shaping as an alternative weighting gives 5.5%/5.5% at 1.5B and is net-negative at 7B. Exact importance sampling is unavailable by construction (the cache carries no likelihoods and need not share a tokenizer); the revision will position DR3 against AWAC/IQL-style advantage weighting, V-trace truncation and prioritized replay, stating which quantities each needs that we lack. For simpler shaping we have no experiment and will not pretend otherwise; the closest evidence is the $-$SC ablation plus a diagnostic on 5,618 saved 3B-ALFWorld rollouts, where the progress value ranks success against failure with AUC 0.83–0.89 on five of six task types. For the camera-ready we commit to two matched-magnitude controls: a task-agnostic potential (step-count/novelty) and a *shuffled* progress map that keeps the bonus scale but destroys its correlation with progress.
