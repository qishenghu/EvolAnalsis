# Response to Reviewer bDeY

Two new results. (i) A code audit confirms your Eq. 9 objection: the implementation applies **one** correction, not $\hat w\rho_t$ — substitution and file:line below. (ii) An executed end-to-end matcher swap (ALFWorld, 30% observation noise): exact matcher **11.0%**, soft matcher over the identical map **54.5%**.

**"Why does Eq. 9 also multiply by $\rho_t$? That double-counts $\hat w$."**
You are right, and the error is ours. The code never forms $\hat w\rho_t$. For teacher samples it *replaces* the behaviour log-prob (`het_actor.py:1507`): $\log\hat\pi_\beta:=\mathrm{sg}[\log\pi_\theta]-\log\hat w(\tau)$, so the single clipped ratio is $\rho^\beta_t=\pi_\theta/\hat\pi_\beta=\hat w(\tau)$ exactly — one correction, not two. $\rho_t$ (Eq. 7) applies to on-policy samples only. Eq. 9 will be rewritten as this substitution. Two further errors we found, both against us: (a) Eq. 8's $D/(1-D)$ is only an intermediate — the applied weight is $\hat w_\alpha=\hat r/((1-\alpha)\hat r+\alpha)\in(0,1/(1-\alpha)]$, measured $\alpha\approx0.10$–$0.12$, so $\hat w\le1.13$ and DR3 can only *down-weight* teacher samples; (b) WebShop configs run a policy-shaping variant (`dr3.use_policy_shaping: true`) that Eq. 9 does not print. **No result changes**; the edits are Eqs. 8–9, an appendix variant, and the contribution statement.

**Claim about being "principled"**
Our own corrections weaken that claim and we will say so. After (a), DR3 is a bounded bias-mitigating replay weight, not an exact likelihood ratio, and it is inert on ALFWorld at 1.5B (47.5% → 47.5%); baseline separation ablates to 0.0% in both settings, i.e. a necessary implementation fix as much as a contribution. "Principled" will describe the *diagnosis* of the two biases and the direction of the corrections, not a derivation; it leaves the abstract, SC is labelled heuristic, and §3.6's non-invariance admission moves into the contribution statement.
The ablation cuts against us too: −SC gives **31.0%** on 1.5B-ALFWorld vs CHORD 27.0% and SFT+GRPO 30.0% — parity, not a win, single seed against a 4.9pp three-seed spread — and on WebShop −SC gives **1.0%**, below CHORD's 11.5%. The defensible claim is narrower: an optional heuristic SC module on a CHORD-compatible core.

**Q2 — does SFT+GRPO use the same teacher data?**
Same cache file, same $n_\text{teacher}{=}1$, same realised per-prompt rate (0.975 vs DUET's 0.978, ALFWorld). **But it is not task-matched: SFT+GRPO trains on 400 distinct tasks against DUET's 800, and consumes ~half the teacher volume (390 vs 782 trajectories).** The headline SFT+GRPO margin is therefore not currently a clean measurement; we are rerunning it on the same 800 tasks. On the curve: the SFT stage executes (ALFWorld on-policy success 0.016 → 0.285 pre-RL, `chord/sft_loss` 0.844 → 0.153), but you are right that training-task success cannot settle this under a 400-vs-800 asymmetry — the denser held-out curve is running.

**One result we should have reported.** From the submitted runs' own logs, at the two evaluation points our protocol already produces: DUET 42.5% → **47.5%** (+5.0), CHORD 30.0% → 27.0% (−3.0), LUFFY 26.0% → 5.5% (−20.5), on-policy GRPO 16.5% → 1.0% (−15.5). Every method that replays or imitates teacher data without our corrections **degrades over the second half of training**; DUET is the only one still improving when the budget ends, and it leads the strongest baseline by 12.5pp at step 50 and 17.5pp at step 100 — the headline does not depend on where we evaluate. This is the "teacher replay is not benign" thesis showing up in the training dynamics rather than in one endpoint.

**Breadth of the evaluation and applicability**
New end-to-end runs, failure case first. With 30% observation noise and **exact** matching, SC coverage falls 0.590 → 0.178 and strict success to **11.0%** — *below* the 31.0% of removing SC entirely, because a matcher that misses scores on-path states as zero rather than abstaining. Under the same noise with a **soft** matcher over the identical progress map: coverage 0.654, success **54.5%**. Same 800 train / 200 val tasks, one config field changed. Missing control: we ran no clean+soft cell, so this shows exact matching is brittle and does **not** establish that soft matching is better (54.5 vs 47.5 is ~1.4 sd of our 4.9pp spread). Your breadth objection otherwise stands: WebShop's $\Phi$ is ~210 lines of hand-written per-environment code — the real cost of SC where SC is load-bearing — and we have no math/reasoning result and will not claim one.

**Q1 — group composition; is $m$ fixed?**
$n{=}7$ on-policy $+\ m{=}1$ teacher $=8$ per prompt. Frozen cache loaded once; sampling without replacement from a fixed per-task list. **No resample-until-$m$-successes loop exists in training** — that loop is in the offline collection script only. A cache miss back-fills on-policy, so the group is always 8. Realised: **0.978** teacher/prompt (ALFWorld), **0.858** (WebShop).

Disclosure we will add: the WebShop entries are verified gold action sequences replayed with 72B-authored rationales, not sampled 72B rollouts.
