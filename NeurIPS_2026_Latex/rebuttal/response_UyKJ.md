# Response to Reviewer UyKJ

Two new measurements answer your hardest questions: a **degraded-teacher experiment** (30% observation noise: 54.5% with a soft matcher, 11.0% with the exact one) and a **cross-family run** (Llama-3.2-3B student, unchanged Qwen-72B cache: 19.5% vs 5.5% on-policy GRPO). We also disclose a 1.5B-WebShop replication failure below.

**Generalization to a different teacher or a non-Qwen student.** Cross-family transfer holds. ALFWorld, **Llama-3.2-3B-Instruct** student, *unchanged* Qwen2.5-72B cache: 19.5% vs 5.5% for on-policy GRPO at the matched step-50 checkpoint, the only validated one (both runs stopped near step 70–78). This pair is **LUFFY, not DUET** (`use_dr3: false`) — labelled, not buried. DUET on Llama is running. On breadth your limitation stands: two environments, one student family; a SciWorld path exists in the repo but has no trained cell and we will not claim one.

**How performance changes when the teacher is weaker.** Two senses, both measured. *Corrupted teacher signal*: with 30% word dropout on the matcher's copy of the observation (policy input untouched), exact hashing retains 6.6% of the clean progress signal and strict success falls end-to-end to **11.0%** — *below* the 31.0% from deleting the State Channel: a missed state scores zero progress, so the shaping misleads rather than abstains. Under the same noise a soft TF-IDF matcher over the identical map retains 101.4% and reaches **54.5%**, against 47.5% clean; same 800 train / 200 val tasks, one config field. *Relatively weaker teacher*: holding the cache fixed and raising the student, the logged teacher–student reward gap goes 0.93/0.55 (early/late) at 1.5B to 0.42/0.06 at 7B, and the teacher's share of the policy gradient falls from a run-mean of 0.241 to 0.079 at an identical mixing rate. Outcome at 7B: DUET 86.5% vs GRPO 85.0%, while LUFFY — same cache, same rate, no fade-out — reaches 82.5%. DUET degrades to GRPO exactly where methods without fade-out fall below the no-teacher baseline.

**What causes the Pick Two exception.** The cell is too small to interpret. Pick Two is n=45 of 200 validation tasks; DUET and GRPO disagree on 18 (6 vs 12), exact paired McNemar **p=0.24**, and three seeds of one *identical* configuration span **8.9pp** on that same subset. Nor does it replicate off the cell: 1.5B DUET 24.4% vs GRPO 0.0%; 7B 77.8% vs 75.6%. One real limitation sits behind it: $\Phi$ is the *maximum* normalized index at which a state occurs, so on Pick Two demonstrations — the most self-revisiting type (52.6% contain a repeated state vs 7.5–30.8%) — the potential saturates inside the first sub-goal. The fix is a sub-goal-indexed potential. That mechanism is measured at 1.5B while the anomalous cell is 3B, whose runs we cannot recompute locally.

**Is the discriminator separating teacher from student, or just success from failure?** Directly tested; it is not a success detector. Probes on DR3's own features (log-prob statistics and length; reward, advantage and success are never inputs) show that restricting the student side to **successes only** does not weaken separation: $D_{\text{succ}}$ = 90.0% on ALFWorld, 99.0% on WebShop late. Late in ALFWorld training, successful and failed student rollouts get near-identical P(student), 0.861 vs 0.869, while teacher trajectories sit at 0.280. WebShop late is the sharpest test: that student already succeeds on 84% of rollouts, and *successful* rollouts score **more** student-like than failed ones (0.947 vs 0.904; teacher 0.155) — the opposite of a success detector. Caveat: an offline probe of DR3's features, not of the online discriminator.

**DR3's theoretical justification.** Eqs. 8–9 misdescribe the implementation; both are corrected in the revision. The applied weight is the $\alpha$-relative ratio $\hat w_\alpha = \hat r/((1-\alpha)\hat r + \alpha)$, bounded by $1/(1-\alpha)\approx 1.13$, so DR3 can only *down-weight* a teacher sample, never amplify one; and for teacher samples the code *replaces* the behaviour log-probability, so the clipped surrogate applies $\hat w_\alpha$ once rather than stacking it on $\rho_t$. Bounded and down-weight-only is what "bias-mitigating replay weight" names; we keep the term.

**Many hyperparameters to tune.** The two channels are not tuned per setting: the SC coefficients and the DR3 discriminator settings are identical across every main-table DUET config. The sharp sensitivity is confined to the **BC cold-start coefficient** ($d_{\text{floor}}$ 0.6→0.5 halves success, →0.4 collapses it) — a safety net, not a channel. At a new scale keep 0.6, with a flat `critic/success_onpolicy/mean` over the first 20 steps as the signal it is too low.

**Disclosure (as given to y9x6).** That BC sensitivity is measured on 1.5B-WebShop, and *that cell reproduces only partially*. Against the submitted **36.0%**, four replicates of the identical configuration — verified field-by-field against the executed config backups, all four DUET mechanisms healthy throughout — give **15.0%** and **24.5%** with the 800-task curriculum pinned to the paper's own draw, and **4.0%** and **2.5%** when the run seed is also allowed to re-draw that curriculum. `data.seed` in this codebase sets both run randomness and task selection, so the latter pair confounds seed with a two-thirds change of training set; we have added `data.task_seed` to separate them. Mechanism, a property of the cell: strict success needs every requested attribute clicked, 88.4% of tasks request ≥2, and every run first converges on a partial-credit 3-action policy that cannot match exactly (0 successes in 10,735 under-clicking episodes), with escape a late stochastic event. We will report the spread, never the best seed.
