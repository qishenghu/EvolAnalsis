\paragraph{Sensitivity discussion.}
The mini-sweep in Table~\ref{tab:app-mu-sensitivity} shows that the $\mu(t)$
schedule is genuinely sensitive on 1.5B-WebShop, especially to
$d_{\text{floor}}$: dropping it from $0.6$ to $0.5$ roughly halves strict
success ($36.0\%\!\rightarrow\!21.5\%$), and dropping it to $0.4$ collapses
it ($2.0\%$). The valley $\mu_{\min}$ has an interior optimum near $0.10$:
$0.05$ loses the late-training anchor ($5.5\%$) and $0.15$ over-regularizes
($2.0\%$). The peak $\mu_{\max}$ is flatter between $0.20$ and $0.40$,
suggesting that peak \emph{duration} (controlled by $d_{\text{floor}}$)
matters more than peak height. The substantive conclusion is that
1.5B-WebShop is sensitive to the duration of the BC safety net, especially
$d_{\text{floor}}$, which is consistent with our framing of BC as a
cold-start safety mechanism rather than a theoretically calibrated quantity.
The companion settings also differ across blocks (in particular the
$\mu_{\max}$ block uses $\mu_{\min}=0.05$ because no clean single-knob
$\mu_{\max}$ sweep was run at the SOTA companion setting). We do not claim
that the schedule is hyperparameter-free or that the SOTA cell sits inside a
wide flat region; the table is a robustness landscape, not a precise
response surface.
