\section{LUFFY reproducibility on 3B-WebShop}
\label{app:luffy_repro}

Table~\ref{tab:main_results} reports LUFFY at $38.0\%$ on 3B-WebShop, taken from our most-careful reproduction on $4 \times$ L20X-144GB. For full transparency, we report all three reproductions we observed:

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
\textbf{Source} & \textbf{Hardware} & \textbf{val@100 SR (strict)} \\
\midrule
\citet{yan2025luffy} (paper)        & not stated            & $49.5\%$  \\
This work                           & $4 \times$ L20X-144GB & $38.0\%$  \\
This work                           & $4 \times$ A100-80GB  & $\phantom{0}3.5\%$ ($\geq 0.9$: $11.5\%$) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Three independent reproductions of LUFFY on 3B-WebShop. Code, configs, teacher data, and seeds are identical across our two reproductions; only the GPU hardware differs.}
\label{tab:luffy_3way}
\end{table}

To localize whether the $34.5$pp cross-infrastructure spread is a property of LUFFY specifically or of the cold-start, sparse-reward 3B-WebShop setting more broadly, we report in Table~\ref{tab:cross_infra} the same 3B-WebShop cell run with DUET on the two hardware configurations.

\begin{table}[h]
\centering
\small
\begin{tabular}{lcc}
\toprule
\textbf{Method (3B-WebShop)} & \textbf{4 $\times$ A100-80GB} & \textbf{4 $\times$ L20X-144GB} \\
\midrule
LUFFY & \phantom{0}$3.5\%$ & $38.0\%$ \\
DUET  &           $44.0\%$ & $45.5\%$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\caption{Cross-infrastructure stability on the same cell. DUET reproduces within $\pm 1.5$pp across the two hardware configurations; LUFFY does not.}
\label{tab:cross_infra}
\end{table}

The cross-infrastructure spread is specific to LUFFY in this comparison: DUET reproduces within $\pm 1.5$pp on the same hardware pair. We were not able to fully isolate the cross-infrastructure root cause within our compute budget; likely candidates include GPU-class differences in numerical precision (BF16 vs.\ FP16 matmul accumulation paths under FSDP), vLLM scheduler timing differences on tensor-parallel rollouts that interact with the asynchronous training loop, and small-magnitude floating-point drift across $100$ training steps that, in a sparse-reward, hard-exploration setting like 3B-WebShop, can determine whether the policy escapes the cold-start basin or not. Whatever the source, the implication for our reported numbers is conservative: we use the LUFFY L20X $38.0\%$ in Table~\ref{tab:main_results}, which is least flattering to DUET's lead.
