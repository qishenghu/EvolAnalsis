## 1. How DR3 w_hat Enters the Gradient (the precise mechanism)

From `het_actor.py:1488`:
```python
old_lp_new[apply_mask] = log_prob.detach()[apply_mask] - log_w[apply_mask]
```

This means:
- `old_log_prob_corrected = log_π_current(a_teacher) - log(w_hat)`
- `ratio = exp(log_π_new - old_log_prob_corrected)`
- At PPO update start (π_new ≈ π_current): **ratio ≈ w_hat**

For teacher samples with w_hat = 0.05, the PPO ratio **starts at 0.05**, not 1.0.

### PPO clip behavior with ratio << 1

Config: `clip_ratio_low = 0.2` → lower clip = 0.8, `off_cliprange_high = 0.6` → upper clip = 1.6

With ratio = 0.05 (well below lower clip 0.8) and positive advantage:
```
loss_unclipped = -adv × 0.05
loss_clipped   = -adv × clamp(0.05, 0.8, 1.6) = -adv × 0.8
max(loss_unclipped, loss_clipped) = loss_unclipped   (less negative → chosen)
```

**The unclipped surrogate dominates when ratio < lower_clip.** Full gradient flows:
```
∂loss/∂θ = -adv × ∂(ratio)/∂θ
```

This is **proportional to advantage magnitude**. The low w_hat doesn't clip the gradient — it just sets the starting point for the ratio. The advantage magnitude determines gradient strength.

### The multiplicative chain

Teacher gradient ∝ advantage × ∂(ratio)/∂θ, where ratio starts at w_hat.

| Component | Original DUET | 0401 (with std floor) |
|-----------|--------------|----------------------|
| w_hat (DR3) | 0.05 | 0.05 |
| advantage | 22,000 (from σ→0) | 0.168 (floored to σ=0.1) |
| gap_gate (if enabled) | ~0.5-1.0 | ~0.5-1.0 |
| **Effective gradient** | **~1,100 × ∂ratio/∂θ** | **~0.008 × ∂ratio/∂θ** |
| vs on-policy gradient | competitive | **negligible** |

The std floor reduces teacher gradient by **~131,000×**. Even with DR3's low w_hat, the original code's advantage explosion compensates. The 0401 code kills both signals simultaneously.

## 3. Why DR3 Discriminator Separates Easily on WebShop

The discriminator uses v3_aug features (`dr3_ratio.py:98-145`):
- `lp_mean, lp_std, lp_min, lp_max` — log-prob statistics
- `lp_low_ratio_10, lp_low_ratio_20, lp_high_ratio_1` — tail ratios
- `resp_len` — response length
- `kl_ref_mean, kl_ref_std, kl_ref_abs_mean, kl_ref_pos_ratio` — KL-to-reference stats

Teacher trajectories are generated by Qwen-72B → their log-prob under the 3B student policy is systematically different (lower lp_mean, higher lp_low_ratio). This creates a **permanently separable** feature space — even as the 3B policy improves on the task, its log-prob profile for teacher actions remains distinct from its own actions.

On ALFWorld: binary rewards + shorter trajectories → less distinctive feature profiles → discriminator has harder time → w_hat stays higher → slower fade-out.

On WebShop: longer, more diverse trajectories + continuous rewards + larger model gap (72B→3B) → clear feature separation → disc_acc → 0.95+ quickly → w_hat → 0 for teacher.

## 4. Evaluation of Proposed DR3 Fixes

### Fix A: Increase disc_temperature (1.5 → 2.5-3.0) — RECOMMENDED

**Mechanism**: Temperature T softens discriminator logits: `d = σ(logit/T)`. Higher T pushes d toward 0.5 → r_hat toward 1.0 → w toward 1.0.

Quantitative effect for teacher sample with raw logit = -4.0 (confident "teacher"):
```
T=1.5: d=0.065, r=0.070, w_α=0.38  (current)
T=2.0: d=0.120, r=0.136, w_α=0.55
T=2.5: d=0.168, r=0.202, w_α=0.63
T=3.0: d=0.209, r=0.266, w_α=0.74
```

**Theoretical justification**: Temperature controls the bias-variance tradeoff of the importance-weighted estimator:
- T→0: exact IS correction (unbiased, high variance from extreme weights)
- T→∞: no correction (biased toward behavior policy, zero-variance)
- Optimal T balances: enough correction to reduce off-policy bias, not so much that teacher signal vanishes

With T=2.5, even a confident discriminator preserves w≈0.63. Combined with advantage explosion (which std floor revert restores), teacher gradient becomes:
- 0.63 × 22,000 × ∂ratio/∂θ = 13,860 × ∂ratio/∂θ (strong signal)

**Pros**:
- Already a parameter (no new hyperparameters)
- Clean theoretical interpretation
- Smooth effect (no discontinuities)
- Preserves DR3's relative ordering (higher-confidence separations still get lower w)

**Cons**:
- Also prevents w from going very high for on-policy samples (minor — w > 1 is rare)
- Requires empirical tuning of the right value

**Verdict: BEST FIX for DR3 specifically. Should be tried at T=2.5 after std floor revert.**

### Fix B: Extended warmup (10 → 30-50) — GOOD PRAGMATIC FIX

**Mechanism**: During warmup, teacher samples use w=1 (LUFFY-style, no importance correction). After warmup, DR3 weighting kicks in.

**Theoretical justification**: The warmup period lets the policy learn from teacher at full strength. By step 30-50, the policy is closer to teacher → discriminator has harder time → w_hat stays higher after warmup ends.

**Pros**:
- Simple, no new hyperparameters
- Already implemented (`apply_warmup_steps` parameter)
- Orthogonal to temperature — can combine both

**Cons**:
- Abrupt transition at warmup boundary (w jumps from 1.0 to w_hat)
- The transition can cause an advantage/gradient discontinuity
- Doesn't help if discriminator still separates easily after warmup

**Verdict: GOOD as complement to temperature increase. Try warmup=30 with T=2.5.**

### Fix C: Lower disc_lr (0.0003 → 0.0001) — ACCEPTABLE

**Mechanism**: Slower discriminator learning → w_hat changes more gradually.

**Theoretical justification**: Reduces discriminator's capacity to track the changing policy distribution. This is a form of "lazy evaluation" — the importance weights lag behind reality.

**Pros**: Easy to tune, slows fade-out trajectory.

**Cons**:
- Doesn't change equilibrium (discriminator eventually converges to same accuracy)
- Delays learning, not prevents it
- Interacts with `disc_steps_per_call=2` and `buffer_size=1024` — may need joint retuning
- A slower discriminator may also be less responsive to policy IMPROVEMENTS (when we want w to increase)

**Verdict: THIRD-LINE option. Only if temperature + warmup aren't enough.**

### Fix D: Min teacher share floor (w_min: 0.01 → 0.15) — AVOID

**Mechanism**: Hard floor on importance weights.

**Theoretical justification**: Guarantees minimum teacher contribution regardless of discriminator confidence.

**Why to avoid**:
- **Breaks IS guarantee permanently**. Unlike temperature (which preserves ordering), a floor creates a discrete transition: below-floor samples all get w=0.15, losing the discriminator's relative ranking information.
- **Doesn't diminish as policy improves**. Even when policy matches teacher, the floor keeps overweighting some teacher samples.
- **Interacts unpredictably with dual ESS clipping**. The dual controller optimizes clip_upper assuming w is from the discriminator distribution; a floor distorts this.
- **The temperature achieves a similar effect more principled** — it preserves ordering and has a smooth, continuous effect.

**Verdict: AVOID. Use temperature instead.**

## 5. Interaction with gap_gate

The config has `gap_gate_enable: true`. From `het_actor.py:1432-1454`:
```python
advantages_used[teacher_sample] = advantages[teacher_sample] * gate_s[teacher_sample]
```

Where `gate_s = teacher_loss_scale.mean(dim=-1).clamp(0, 1)`. This is ANOTHER multiplier on teacher advantages.

The full chain becomes:
```
teacher_gradient ∝ advantage × gap_gate × ∂ratio/∂θ
where ratio starts at w_hat
```

If gap_gate is also < 1 (e.g., 0.5), this further suppresses teacher gradient:
- With std floor: 0.168 × 0.5 × ∂ratio/∂θ = 0.084 (dead)
- Without std floor: 22,000 × 0.5 × ∂ratio/∂θ = 11,000 (still strong, PPO clips)

**Question for exp-analyst**: What is `dr3/gap_gate_mean` over training? If it's consistently < 0.3, it may be compounding the fade-out issue independently of std floor.

## 7. Theoretical Framework for the Paper

This diagnosis reveals a clean story about teacher influence in DUET:

**Three-layer teacher influence control** (emergent, not designed):
1. **DR3 w_hat** (density ratio): controls the PPO ratio starting point. High discriminator accuracy → low w_hat → ratio starts far below 1.
2. **GRPO advantage normalization**: σ→0 creates explosive advantages. PPO clipping bounds the update magnitude, but the direction is maximally toward teacher.
3. **Gap gate** (if enabled): explicit reward-gap-based modulation.

The interaction between layers 1 and 2 creates a natural curriculum:
- Early: w_hat moderate, advantage moderate → balanced learning
- Mid: w_hat drops (discriminator learns), but advantage grows (σ shrinks) → partially compensating
- Late: w_hat very low, advantage very high → the explosion compensates for low w_hat

**The std floor broke layer 2, leaving only layer 1 (which fades too fast on its own).**

This is a novel observation about the interaction between GRPO normalization and importance-weighted policy optimization. Worth a subsection in the paper.
